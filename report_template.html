How to Read This Report

What this shows

Each condition in your strategy (RSI check, price/MA cross, etc.) is tested across a range of slightly different parameter values — the "fuzz". If the condition only works at one very specific value, it may be overfit. If it works across a broad range, it's likely robust.

The charts

Heatmap (2D conditions): rows = indicator period, columns = threshold value. Each cell's colour shows win rate: red = low, green = high. A healthy condition shows a broad green plateau. A spike of green surrounded by red is a warning sign.

Line chart (1D conditions): Used for MA crosses and RSI-vs-RSI where there's no threshold to sweep — only the window length varies. A flat line = robust. A sharp peak = fragile.

Detail charts (click any condition to open)

Clicking a condition or heatmap cell loads four charts in the right panel. Each reflects the currently selected parameter values — clicking a different cell re-renders them for that cell's period and threshold.

Equity Curves
Strategy (green), Buy-and-Hold (blue), and BIL (grey dashed) all indexed to 100 at the start of the data. Green shaded bands mark days the signal was ON. Ideally the strategy line rises during ON bands and stays flat or tracks BIL during OFF periods.
Price & Signal Overlay
The allocated asset's price, normalized to 100 at the start of the data. Green dots mark individual signal-ON days; green bands span continuous ON periods. Use this to see what the market was doing when the condition fired — are the triggers clustered in obvious trends, or scattered randomly?
Indicator Values
The raw indicator value (e.g. RSI, cumulative return, moving average) plotted over time. A horizontal red dashed line shows the threshold if the condition compares against a fixed value. Green bands again show signal-ON periods. Use this to verify the indicator is actually crossing the threshold when you expect it to.
Profile
Win rate (green, left axis) and profit factor (blue dashed, right axis) across the parameter sweep for the selected period. The orange dashed line marks the base parameter value from your strategy. A wide, flat plateau around the orange line means the condition is robust — the exact value doesn't matter much. A sharp spike directly on the orange line is a red flag for overfitting.

Allocation tabs

When a condition gates multiple possible holdings, each gets its own tab. The sweep for each tab measures whether that specific asset outperforms BIL on days the condition fired.

Metrics

Win Rate
% of days the condition fired where the allocated asset returned more than BIL. >55% is meaningful; >65% is strong.
Profit Factor
Sum of gains ÷ sum of losses on signal days. >1.0 means the wins outweigh the losses in dollar terms. >1.5 is solid.
Trades (n)
How many days the condition fired. Low n (under ~20) means the win rate is unreliable — small samples swing wildly.
Score
Win Rate × log(n). Penalises high win rates that come from tiny samples. Useful for ranking conditions against each other.
vs [asset]
% of signal days where the allocated asset beat the selected comparison asset. Change the asset via the "vs" dropdown at the top of the report. Relevant when the condition is supposed to be an improvement over just holding a benchmark.

Fragility score (sidebar colours)

Robust
Win rate is very consistent across the sweep. High confidence the condition is real.
Stable
Minor variation. Probably fine; worth a quick look.
Moderate
Noticeable variation. The exact parameter values matter more than you'd like.
Fragile
Win rate spikes sharply at the fitted value. Consider widening the parameter or removing the condition.
Very Fragile
The condition appears heavily overfit. Treat with scepticism.

Tail score (sidebar "T:" badge)

The tail score answers a different question from fragility: even at the base parameters, does this condition's edge come from a handful of extreme return days — or from consistent outperformance? A high tail score means the win rate collapses if you strip out the best few days, which is a sign the condition is luck-dependent rather than structural.

T: Robust (0–0.30)
Edge is spread across many ordinary days. The condition holds up even after removing outliers.
T: Moderate (0.30–0.55)
Some tail dependency. A few big days contribute disproportionately, but the edge is not entirely concentrated.
T: Tail-driven (0.55+)
The edge is heavily concentrated in outlier days. Strip out the top 5% of returns and the win rate drops sharply — treat with caution.

Combined score and sidebar ranking

Conditions are ranked within each sub-strategy by Combined = 0.6 × Fragility + 0.4 × Tail Score. The most problematic conditions appear first. A condition can rank high because it is parameter-sensitive (high fragility), tail-dependent (high tail score), or both.

Tail Analysis panel (pin a cell to open)

Clicking a cell in the heatmap pins it and opens the Tail Analysis panel in the right column. All values reflect the base parameter point from your strategy, not the specific cell you clicked.

Tail Score
Combined measure of how concentrated the edge is in outlier days (0 = fully distributed, 1 = entirely tail-driven).
Tail Concentration
Fraction of total outperformance that comes from just the top 5% of return days. 0.5 means half the edge comes from the best 5% of days.
Return Kurtosis
Excess kurtosis of the signal-day returns. High kurtosis (>3) means the return distribution has heavy tails — a few very large or very small days dominate.
Win Rate (base)
% of signal days where the allocated asset beat BIL, at the strategy's fitted parameter values.
Win Rate (stripped)
Same win rate after removing the top 5% of return days. If this drops sharply from the base win rate, the edge depends on catching occasional big moves.
WR Delta
Base win rate minus stripped win rate. >10 percentage points is a significant tail dependency; >20 pp is a red flag.

Comparing cells

Click any cell to pin it (blue border). Click a second cell to pin it (yellow border). The right panel shows both sets of stats plus a Δ row showing the difference.
